# Reference candidates for §7.2

Candidates below were checked against DOI-linked publisher records during the
web search on 2026-07-03. “Verified” means that the DOI and bibliographic record

## Slot 1 — HSR/Shinkansen tourism-impact evaluations

1. Hashimoto, T., Hayakawa, Y., & Suzuki, A. (2017). Analysis on change of
   visitors before and after the open of high speed rail using mobile spatial
   statistics. *Journal of the Eastern Asia Society for Transportation Studies,
   12*, 959–974. https://doi.org/10.11175/easts.12.959
   - Relevance: Direct before/after analysis of the March 2015 Hokuriku
     Shinkansen extension, including visitor changes in Kanazawa and Toyama.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI and article metadata displayed by J-STAGE
     (Eastern Asia Society for Transportation Studies).

2. Yamamoto, S. (2016). Hokuriku Shinkansen kaigyō no ryokōsha dōtai e no
   eikyō no kōsatsu [The impact on tourists by the Hokuriku Shinkansen].
   *Nagoya Gakuin University Review of Social Sciences, 53*(1), 77–84.
   https://doi.org/10.15012/00000749
   - Relevance: Japan-specific empirical study using mobile-location data to
     examine tourist movements in Ishikawa and Toyama after the 2015 opening.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI and repository record displayed by CiNii
     Research and the Nagoya Gakuin University repository.

3. Li, L. S. Z., Yang, F. X., & Cui, C. (2019). High-speed rail and tourism in
   China: An urban agglomeration perspective. *International Journal of Tourism
   Research, 21*(1), 45–60. https://doi.org/10.1002/jtr.2240
   - Relevance: Uses panel data and synthetic controls to estimate heterogeneous
     HSR effects on domestic and international tourist arrivals.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI, authors, volume, and pages displayed on
     the Wiley journal page.

## Slot 2 — Tourism satisfaction and friction survey studies

1. Žabkar, V., Brenčič, M. M., & Dmitrović, T. (2010). Modelling perceived
   quality, visitor satisfaction and behavioural intentions at the destination
   level. *Tourism Management, 31*(4), 537–546.
   https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tourman.2009.06.005
   - Relevance: Survey of 1,056 visitors across four destinations links
     destination attributes and perceived quality to satisfaction and intended
     behaviour.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI and full journal metadata displayed on
     Elsevier's ScienceDirect record.

2. Bernini, C., & Cagnone, S. (2014). Analysing tourist satisfaction at a
   mature and multi-product destination. *Current Issues in Tourism, 17*(1),
   1–20. https://doi.org/10.1080/13683500.2012.702737
   - Relevance: Uses repeated tourist-satisfaction surveys to identify which
     destination attributes and service conditions drive overall satisfaction.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI, authors, issue, and pages displayed on
     the Taylor & Francis article page.

3. Sánchez-Rebull, M.-V., Rudchenko, V., & Martín, J. C. (2018). The
   antecedents and consequences of customer satisfaction in tourism: A
   systematic literature review. *Tourism and Hospitality Management, 24*(1),
   151–183. https://doi.org/10.20867/thm.24.1.3
   - Relevance: Synthesizes survey-based evidence on service quality,
     expectations, perceived value, emotions, and other sources of tourism
     satisfaction or friction.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI, authors, journal, volume, and pages
     displayed in the journal bibliographic record.

## Slot 3 — Behavioural interventions and nudges in tourism

1. Goldstein, N. J., Cialdini, R. B., & Griskevicius, V. (2008). A room with a
   viewpoint: Using social norms to motivate environmental conservation in
   hotels. *Journal of Consumer Research, 35*(3), 472–482.
   https://doi.org/10.1086/586910
   - Relevance: Two hotel field experiments test descriptive and
     context-specific social-norm messages on guests' towel reuse.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI resolved during this search to the Oxford
     Academic journal record with matching authors, volume, issue, and pages.

2. Kallbekken, S., & Sælen, H. (2013). “Nudging” hotel guests to reduce food
   waste as a win–win environmental measure. *Economics Letters, 119*(3),
   325–327. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econlet.2013.03.019
   - Relevance: Hotel field experiment finds that smaller plates and a social
     cue each reduced buffet food waste.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI and complete article metadata displayed
     on Elsevier's ScienceDirect record.

3. Bohner, G., & Schlüter, L. E. (2014). A room with a viewpoint revisited:
   Descriptive norms and hotel guests' towel reuse behavior. *PLOS ONE, 9*(8),
   e104086. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0104086
   - Relevance: Two hotel field experiments replicate and probe boundary
     conditions of social-norm nudges, providing useful mixed-result evidence.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI resolved during this search to the
     peer-reviewed PLOS article with matching citation metadata.

## Slot 4 — Two-stage/internal-pilot and adaptive sample-size designs

   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI and full citation displayed on Wiley's
     journal page.

   15*(4), 559–574. https://doi.org/10.1081/BIP-200062852

     nuisance parameters before final sample-size revision.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI, author, issue, and pages displayed on
     the Taylor & Francis article page.

3. Friede, T., & Kieser, M. (2006). Sample size recalculation in internal pilot

   - Relevance: Reviews blinded and unblinded internal-pilot recalculation
     methods, including implications for power and type-I error.
   - Verification: **Verified** — DOI and matching citation displayed by
     PubMed and Wiley.
